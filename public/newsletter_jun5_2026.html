Board of Supervisors · Week of June 5, 2026
The big picture
Supervisors advanced major capital and infrastructure financing, including a $195 million general obligation bond spending plan and nearly $1.9 billion in Public Utilities Commission bond authority on first reading. The Board also approved large, multi-year contracts supporting public health operations and permanent supportive housing, and moved forward on new entertainment zones in North Beach and around the Ferry Building. Most votes were unanimous, with one supervisor excused and one notable 9-1 vote on a resolution supporting state legislation for camera-based enforcement in transit lanes and bikeways.

Top items
IN PROGRESS
Board advances $195 million bond spending plan
Supervisors approved on first reading a plan to spend $195M in voter-approved general obligation bond proceeds for healthcare and behavioral health facilities, street and pedestrian safety projects, and public space improvements. The funding is allocated across the Department of Public Health, Public Works, Recreation and Park, and the San Francisco Municipal Transportation Agency.
This is a major citywide capital investment package, affecting health facilities and transportation safety projects. Final approval and the timing of project delivery will shape when residents see on-the-ground improvements.
Passed on first reading, 10-0 (one supervisor excused).
IN PROGRESS
City authorizes up to $1.87 billion in utility bonds
Supervisors approved on first reading bond authority for the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission to finance major capital work across wastewater, water, and power systems. The measures set maximum borrowing amounts for each system and are intended to support long-term infrastructure projects.
These are among the largest dollar items on the agenda and underpin long-term reliability for water, sewer, and power infrastructure. Borrowing decisions can also influence future ratepayer costs, though the minutes do not detail rate impacts.
Three bond ordinances passed on first reading, each 10-0 (one supervisor excused).
PASSED
Public health supply contracts expanded through 2030
The Board approved amendments with Medline Industries, Inc. to extend and expand two major supply agreements for medical, surgical, and laboratory items used by the Department of Public Health. The changes extend both contracts through June 30, 2030 and increase the not-to-exceed totals.
These contracts support day-to-day operations across the public health system, including clinics and other facilities that rely on consistent medical supplies. The scale of the agreements makes them a significant part of the City’s ongoing health operations.
Two resolutions adopted, 10-0 (one supervisor excused).
PASSED
Tenderloin Housing Clinic contract grows to $303.6 million
Supervisors approved an amendment to extend and expand the Department of Homelessness and Supportive Housing’s grant agreement with Tenderloin Housing Clinic for property management and supportive services at 16 permanent supportive housing sites. The amendment extends the term to December 31, 2027 and increases the total not-to-exceed amount.
This funding supports ongoing operations at a large portfolio of permanent supportive housing, a core part of the City’s homelessness response. Contract continuity affects building operations, resident services, and housing stability for tenants.
Adopted, 10-0 (one supervisor excused).
PASSED
Crisis Stabilization Unit contract extended at 822 Geary
The Board approved an amendment with Crestwood Behavioral Health Inc. to continue operating a Crisis Stabilization Unit at 822 Geary. The amendment extends the agreement through June 30, 2028 and increases the not-to-exceed amount.
Crisis stabilization capacity is a key part of the City’s behavioral health safety net, offering an alternative to emergency rooms for urgent mental health needs. The specific site at 822 Geary is explicitly tied to the service in the minutes.
Adopted, 10-0 (one supervisor excused).
