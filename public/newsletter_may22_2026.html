Board of Supervisors · Week of May 22, 2026
The big picture
Supervisors approved major downtown and governance actions, including creating a new Downtown Hospitality Zone and advancing a sweeping overhaul of city commissions and advisory bodies on a close 6-4 vote. The Board also moved significant money to cover projected overtime in emergency-related departments and authorized substantial financial support for the San Francisco Zoo. Several large health and airport contracts were introduced and sent to committee, setting up consequential follow-on votes.

New Downtown Hospitality Zone approved for core area
The Board approved an ordinance creating a Downtown Hospitality Zone with boundaries spanning parts of SoMa, Union Square, and the downtown core. The legislation also affirmed the Planning Department’s environmental review determination.
The new zone sets a defined geography for downtown hospitality-focused rules and programs, which can shape how the City manages activity and services in the area. Residents and businesses inside the boundaries should expect future implementation details to flow from this designation.
Passed unanimously, 10-0 (one supervisor excused).
PASSED
Fire, emergency management, and utilities overtime funding approved
The Board shifted funding to cover projected overtime costs, including new revenue in the Fire Department and moving money out of some permanent salary budgets in multiple departments. The changes direct more funding to overtime in the Fire Department, the Department of Emergency Management, and the Public Utilities Commission.
Overtime funding affects staffing capacity for emergency response and critical city services. The vote signals the City expects higher overtime needs through the fiscal year and is reallocating funds to meet them.
Finally passed unanimously, 10-0 (one supervisor excused).
PASSED
City authorizes up to $8.5 million loan to Zoo
The Board authorized the Recreation and Park Department to loan up to $8.5 million to the San Francisco Zoological Society to support operations and long-term financial sustainability. The loan is structured to be repaid over 10 years through deductions from the society’s management fee, with the City able to terminate the lease and management agreement if conditions are not met.
This is a major financial backstop for a high-profile public attraction, with repayment and performance conditions attached. It also increases the City’s leverage over the Zoo’s management if the loan terms are not met.
Adopted unanimously, 10-0 (one supervisor excused).
PASSED
Board redirects $2.5 million for separate Zoo loan
The Board moved $2.5 million out of Open Space Acquisition funding and into the Recreation and Park Department to support a loan to the San Francisco Zoological Society in fiscal year 2025-2026.
Redirecting funds can delay or reduce other open-space purchases while prioritizing near-term support for Zoo finances. The action also pairs with the larger loan authorization approved the same day.
Finally passed unanimously, 10-0 (one supervisor excused).
PASSED
Commission and advisory body overhaul passes on 6-4 vote
The Board approved a wide-ranging ordinance that standardizes rules for city commissions and advisory bodies, including terms, term limits, and limits on holdover service. It also sunsets, abolishes, renames, or reclassifies certain bodies; adds two state-required councils; and consolidates many provisions into one section of the Administrative Code.
This reshapes how many city boards and advisory groups are structured and maintained, which can affect public oversight and how residents participate in city decision-making. The close vote underscores that the changes were contested.
Finally passed 6-4 (one supervisor excused).
