Board of Supervisors · Week of May 1, 2026
The big picture
Supervisors approved a major package of federal housing and homelessness grants for 2026–27 and advanced a sweeping zoning ordinance setting San Francisco’s approach to state transit-oriented housing rules. The Board also moved forward on public safety staffing with a federal police hiring grant and approved new entertainment zones in Glen Park and Upper Fillmore. Several big-ticket labor agreements and policy changes cleared first votes and are now headed toward final approval.

PASSED
Federal housing and homelessness grants approved for 2026–27
The Board approved San Francisco’s annual federal housing and community development grant plans and authorized the Mayor to apply for, accept, and spend the funds. The package includes Community Development Block Grant, HOME Investment Partnership, Emergency Solutions Grants, and Housing Opportunities for Persons with AIDS funding.
These federal dollars help fund affordable housing, homelessness services, and neighborhood investments across San Francisco. Approval keeps funding on track for the fiscal year starting July 1, 2026.
Adopted unanimously (10-0; one Supervisor excused).
IN PROGRESS
Police hiring grant approved to add 50 officers
The Board retroactively authorized the Police Department to accept and spend a $6.25 million U.S. Department of Justice Community Oriented Policing Services grant. The funding supports 50 new police officer positions for about one year, and the Board approved adding the grant-funded positions to the City’s staffing plan.
This decision expands sworn staffing using federal funds, with implications for public safety coverage and future budget choices once grant funding ends.
Passed on first reading unanimously (10-0; one Supervisor excused).
IN PROGRESS
Major zoning changes set local rules near transit
The Board advanced a wide-ranging Planning Code ordinance responding to California Senate Bill 79, including temporary and permanent exclusions for certain sites and added allowances for housing density and height on some parcels near transit.
The ordinance shapes where housing can be built near transit and where industrial areas are shielded from state upzoning rules.
Passed on first reading as amended (10-0; one Supervisor excused).
PASSED
Two new entertainment zones approved in Glen Park and Fillmore
The Board created the Glen Park Entertainment Zone and the Upper Fillmore Entertainment Zone, setting designated corridors where entertainment-zone rules apply. The Glen Park zone covers Diamond Street, Chenery Street, Monterey Boulevard, and Kern Street; the Upper Fillmore zone covers Fillmore Street from Jackson to Sutter plus portions of California and Pine to Steiner.
Entertainment zones can change how streets are managed for events and nightlife, affecting nearby residents and businesses. The approvals formalize these corridors as priority areas for activation and programming.
Both ordinances finally passed unanimously (10-0; one Supervisor excused).
