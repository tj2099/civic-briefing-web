The big picture

The Board also approved a new Fire Code Technical Advisory Council for decisions affecting some sprinklered high-rise residential buildings, and advanced several land-use and neighborhood economic measures on first reading, including the One Oak Street height change and new entertainment zones.

IN PROGRESS
Airport ride-hail virtual queue surveillance policy sent back
The Board voted to send an ordinance back to committee that would approve the San Francisco International Airport surveillance technology policy for a transportation network company “virtual queue” system. Supervisors cited unresolved questions about privacy and real-world impacts on drivers, including immigrant drivers, and asked for fuller public vetting in committee.
This pauses adoption of rules governing how ride-hail queue technology would be used at the airport, including what data may be collected and how it is handled. The next committee hearing is expected to surface privacy safeguards and labor impacts before the Board considers final approval.
Referred back to committee without objection.
PASSED
New Fire Code advisory council approved for high-rises
The Board approved creating a Fire Code Technical Advisory Council to advise the Fire Marshal on what evidence to consider when deciding whether to waive, modify, or delay certain Fire Code compliance requirements for some existing high-rise residential buildings with sprinkler systems.
For residents of older high-rises, this could shape how quickly and under what conditions buildings must meet certain Fire Code requirements. The new council adds technical review and consistency to decisions that affect safety, costs, and timelines for building compliance.
Finally passed 8-1, with Supervisor Walton voting no.
PASSED
Public Utilities Commission contract approved for grid compliance work
The Board authorized the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission to enter a contract with Archer Energy Solutions LLC for as-needed professional services supporting Hetch Hetchy Water and Power compliance with multiple electric reliability and regulatory requirements. The work runs from May 2026 through May 2031.
This funding supports technical work tied to keeping San Francisco’s publicly owned power system in compliance with reliability rules, helping avoid penalties and reduce operational risk for critical city infrastructure.
Adopted without objection for $11.6 million.
PASSED
Election system replacement funding accepted from the state
The Board retroactively authorized the Department of Elections to accept and spend state funds to support replacement of the voting system and election management system. The funding covers July 1, 2025 through August 31, 2026.
Election technology underpins ballot processing, reporting, and election administration. This allows the city to use state funds already allocated for modernization work.
Adopted without objection for about $416K.
IN PROGRESS
One Oak Street project cleared for taller building podium
The Board advanced a zoning map change for the One Oak Street project, increasing the allowed height for the building’s podium from 120 feet to 140 feet.
If finally approved, the change allows a larger podium massing than current rules permit, affecting the project’s design and neighborhood scale.
Passed on first reading without objection.
