Board of Supervisors · Week of June 26, 2026
The big picture
Supervisors approved the City’s interim two-year budget and staffing plan and passed a new fire-safety rule banning the sale and delivery of uncertified lithium-ion batteries to San Francisco addresses. The Board also revised a high-profile downtown lease at 44 Ellis Street, authorized private fundraising to expand shelter and homeless services, and advanced updates to the domestic violence shelter fund that still need a final vote.

PASSED
Interim two-year city budget and staffing plan approved
The Board gave final approval to the City’s interim budget for fiscal years 2026-2027 and 2027-2028, covering expected revenues and department spending. It also approved the interim salary and position ordinance that sets out City-funded jobs, pay, and work schedules for the same period.
This action keeps City services funded and positions authorized at the start of the new fiscal year while the broader budget process continues. It affects operations across departments citywide.
Both measures were finally passed 10-0, with one supervisor excused.
PASSED
Board bans sale and delivery of uncertified batteries
The Board amended the San Francisco Fire Code to prohibit the sale, offer, and delivery of lithium-ion batteries and replacement batteries that do not meet specified certification requirements to any address in San Francisco. The ordinance also sets enforcement and penalties, and authorizes the City Attorney and Fire Department to pursue compliance.
The measure is aimed at reducing fire risk linked to unsafe batteries, with direct implications for consumers, delivery platforms, and retailers operating in San Francisco. Enforcement authority and penalties could change how products are sold and shipped into the city.
Finally passed 10-0, with one supervisor excused.
PASSED
City approves revised Tad’s lease at 44 Ellis
The Board retroactively approved changes to the City’s lease with Tad’s Inc. for retail space in the Ellis-O’Farrell Garage at 44 Ellis Street. The changes shift rent to 8% of gross revenues, adjust water charges, set minimum operating hours, add a $4K administrative fee, and waive certain past rent and water charges for specified periods.
This restructures how the City collects revenue from a prominent downtown storefront and resolves several retroactive billing and waiver issues. It also sets expectations for hours of operation through the lease term ending April 30, 2029.
Adopted 10-0, with one supervisor excused.
PASSED
Board authorizes private fundraising for shelter expansion
The Board approved a waiver allowing senior leaders at the Department of Homelessness and Supportive Housing to solicit donations from private entities to support expansion of temporary shelter and other homeless services, notwithstanding the City’s behested payment rules.
This expands the City’s ability to seek private support for homelessness response efforts. It also raises transparency and governance considerations because it operates through an exception to standard solicitation restrictions.
Adopted 10-0, with one supervisor excused.
IN PROGRESS
Domestic violence shelter fund renamed and updated
The Board amended City law to rename and revise the purpose of the domestic violence program fund, and to designate the Mayor’s Office of Housing and Community Development (or another mayor-designated agency) as the fund administrator. The Board also updated provisions to reflect changes in state law related to fees collected by the County Clerk.
If approved on final reading, the changes would update how the fund is managed and align local rules with state requirements. The next step is a final vote at a future Board meeting.
Passed on first reading as amended, 10-0, with one supervisor excused.
