Board of Supervisors · Week of June 26, 2026
The big picture
The Board's most consequential action on June 23 was unanimous passage of the city's interim two-year budget and salary ordinance, setting spending and pay for all city departments through June 2028. The Board also finalized a citywide ban on uncertified lithium-ion batteries and cleared homeless services officials to solicit private donations for shelter expansion — a move that drew public scrutiny over transparency. Several pending items, including a Mission District landmark designation and two tax refund settlements, move to committee for further review.

PASSED
City adopts interim two-year budget and salary plan for all departments
The Board unanimously passed two companion measures: an interim spending plan covering all city department budgets, and a companion salary ordinance setting positions and pay scales for the fiscal years ending June 2027 and June 2028.
These two measures together govern how the City and County of San Francisco spends public money and pays its workforce for the next two fiscal years — touching every city department, every city employee, and every resident who depends on city services.
Both measures finally passed 10-0, with Supervisor Fielder excused.
PASSED
San Francisco bans uncertified lithium-ion batteries citywide
The Board unanimously passed an ordinance amending the city's Fire Code to prohibit the sale, delivery, and offer of lithium-ion batteries — including replacement batteries — that do not meet specified safety certification standards. The Fire Department is authorized to enforce the ban through rules and guidance, and the City Attorney may seek court orders and financial penalties against violators.
Uncertified lithium-ion batteries have been linked to fires in e-bikes, scooters, and other devices across San Francisco. This ordinance gives the city direct enforcement tools — including the ability to seek injunctions and monetary penalties — to remove unsafe batteries from the market before they cause harm.
Finally passed 10-0; the ban applies to all addresses within San Francisco.
PASSED
City officials cleared to solicit private donations for homeless shelter expansion
The Board adopted a resolution allowing senior leaders at the Department of Homelessness and Supportive Housing — including the Executive Director, Chief Deputies, Deputy Directors, and Program Directors — to solicit private donations from companies and organizations to fund expanded temporary shelter and other homeless services, bypassing the city's standard rules that restrict such solicitations.
The city's standard ethics rules — known as the Behested Payment Ordinance — restrict public officials from directing private entities to make donations, in part to prevent the appearance of pay-to-play influence. A public commenter at the meeting specifically raised concerns about transparency. This waiver allows the homeless services department to tap private funding to expand shelter capacity, but critics argue it reduces public accountability over who is donating and why.
Adopted 10-0.
PASSED
Tad's Steakhouse locks in restructured lease at Ellis-O'Farrell Garage
The Board approved a revised lease agreement between the city and Tad's Inc. for the restaurant's retail space at 44 Ellis Street, inside the city-owned Ellis-O'Farrell Garage. The deal restructures rent from a fixed monthly amount to 8 percent of gross revenues, retroactive to January 1, 2021. It also waives Tad's obligation to pay base rent from April through December 2020 — the early pandemic period — and forgives outstanding water charges from February 2020 through January 2024. Going forward, Tad's will pay a monthly water charge of $2.4K and a one-time $1.4K administrative fee.
The restructured lease reflects the financial strain the pandemic placed on downtown San Francisco businesses. By tying rent to a share of revenue rather than a fixed amount, the city accepts more risk but gives Tad's a path to stay open — preserving a longtime presence in a downtown corridor that has struggled with vacancies since 2020.
Adopted 10-0; the agreement runs through April 30, 2029.
IN PROGRESS
Domestic violence shelter fund renamed and given broader scope
The Board gave first-round approval to an ordinance renaming the Status of Women Domestic Violence Program Fund to the Domestic Violence Shelter-Based Program Fund, revising its stated purpose, and shifting administrative oversight to the Mayor's Office of Housing and Community Development. The measure also updates the fund's rules to reflect recent changes in state law governing fees collected by the County Clerk.
The fund supports shelter-based programs for domestic violence survivors. The name change and administrative shift signal an intent to align the fund more closely with housing and community development resources — though the practical effect on funding levels or service delivery is not specified in the minutes.
Passed on first reading as amended, 10-0; requires a second vote to become law.
