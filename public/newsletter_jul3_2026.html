Board of Supervisors · Week of July 3, 2026
The big picture
The June 30 Board meeting was dominated by two high-stakes items headed for future votes: a proposed charter amendment to create a San Francisco public bank, continued to July 7 with only Supervisor Wong dissenting, and a sweeping city code overhaul touching more than 100 programs, pushed to July 14. The Board also unanimously scheduled a July 21 hearing to scrutinize Kaiser Permanente's labor negotiations, reflecting concern about the impact on City employees and behavioral health services. On the consent side, the City approved a $3 million medical negligence settlement and gave final passage to a renamed domestic violence shelter fund.
Top items
IN PROGRESS
Public bank and municipal finance corporation headed to November ballot
The Board took up a proposed charter amendment that would authorize the City to establish a Municipal Finance Corporation and a Public Bank as nonprofit corporations, with defined mission, principles, and governance structure. The item was continued by a 10-1 vote to the July 7 meeting for further consideration.
If the Board approves the charter amendment and voters ratify it in November 2026, San Francisco would create its own public bank — a significant shift in how the city could finance affordable housing, infrastructure, and other public priorities outside of traditional commercial lenders.
Continued to July 7, 2026; passed 10-1, with Supervisor Wong casting the sole no vote.
IN PROGRESS
City to pay $3 million to settle medical negligence lawsuit
The Board approved on first reading a settlement of $3 million to Amaryllis Cruz and Elias Jimenez, who sued the City over alleged medical negligence. The lawsuit was in September 2023.
At $3 million, this is the largest single lawsuit settlement on the agenda and reflects ongoing liability exposure for the City in medical care contexts. A second Board vote is required before the settlement is finalized.
Passed on first reading, 11-0; requires a second reading to take final effect.
IN PROGRESS
Board schedules Kaiser Permanente labor hearing for July 21
The Board voted to convene a full Committee of the Whole hearing on July 21, 2026, to examine Kaiser Permanente's proposals in contract negotiations with the National Union of Healthcare Workers. The hearing will focus on potential impacts on San Francisco residents enrolled in Kaiser, City employees covered by Kaiser, behavioral health clinicians, and standards of care. Kaiser's chief executives and union representatives were requested to appear.
Kaiser Permanente is one of the largest health insurers for City employees and San Francisco residents. The Board's intervention signals concern that Kaiser's bargaining positions could reduce access to behavioral health services — a particularly sensitive issue given the city's ongoing mental health crisis.
Motion approved unanimously, 11-0; hearing set for July 21, 2026, at 3:00 p.m.
IN PROGRESS
Fair Chance Ordinance expanded to shield abortion and gender-affirming care records
The Board passed on first reading an amendment to the Fair Chance Ordinance barring employers and affordable housing providers from using out-of-state criminal convictions or arrests for conduct that is legal in California — specifically abortion-related healthcare, drag performances, gender-affirming care, and spontaneous abortion — when making hiring or housing decisions. The amendment also increases financial penalties for violations.
As other states continue to criminalize abortion and gender-affirming care, this amendment would prevent San Francisco employers and landlords from penalizing residents whose out-of-state records reflect those prosecutions — offering a concrete legal protection for affected individuals seeking work or housing in the city.
Passed on first reading, 11-0; requires a second reading to take final effect.
IN PROGRESS
City's Microsoft software contract expanded to $115 million through 2029
The Department of Technology introduced a resolution to increase the City's contract with Zones, LLC for Microsoft enterprise software products by $60 million, bringing the total contract ceiling to $115 million, and extending the agreement by three years through August 2029.
At $115 million, this is the largest single contract introduced at this meeting and covers the software infrastructure that underpins City operations across dozens of departments. The Budget and Finance Committee will scrutinize the terms before the full Board votes.
Introduced and referred to the Budget and Finance Committee for review.
