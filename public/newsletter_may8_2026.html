Board of Supervisors · Week of May 8, 2026
The big picture
Supervisors approved major spending for in-home supportive services, including a nearly $1 billion agreement, and advanced a $6.25 million federal grant to hire 50 new police officers. The Board also set local rules for transit-adjacent housing growth under state law and authorized a new curbside electric vehicle charging permit program. Several significant items—including a Public Defender funding boost and a proposed shift toward drug-free supportive housing—were left for final action in the coming week.

PASSED
Nearly $1 billion approved for in-home supportive services
The Board approved a five-year agreement for independent-provider in-home supportive services through the Department of Disability and Aging Services and the San Francisco In-Home Supportive Services Public Authority. The Board also approved an amendment increasing the City’s contract-mode in-home supportive services agreement with Homebridge, Inc.
These contracts fund core services that help seniors and people with disabilities remain safely housed and supported at home. The scale of the spending makes this one of the meeting’s biggest citywide service decisions.
Both resolutions were adopted 9-0 (with 2 supervisors excused).
PASSED
Police hiring grant approved for 50 new officers
The Board retroactively authorized the San Francisco Police Department to accept and spend a federal grant from the United States Department of Justice’s Office of Community Oriented Policing Services. The funding is intended to support 50 new police officer positions for about one year, and the Board approved adding the grant-funded positions to the City’s staffing plan.
This decision increases sworn staffing capacity using federal dollars, with implications for response times and neighborhood policing. The minutes are unclear on how the one-year hiring support aligns with the longer date range listed in the ordinance.
Finally passed 9-0 (with 2 supervisors excused).
PASSED
New labor agreements approved for police and firefighters
The Board adopted new multi-year labor agreements between the City and the San Francisco Police Officers’ Association, and between the City and San Francisco Fire Fighters Union Local 798 for two bargaining units. The agreements are set to take effect July 1, 2026.
Labor agreements shape public safety staffing, pay, and working conditions for years, and can significantly affect the City budget. The minutes do not summarize the contract terms beyond the effective dates.
All three agreements were finally passed 9-0 (with 2 supervisors excused).
PASSED
Board sets local rules for transit-adjacent housing growth
The Board approved changes to the Planning Code responding to California Senate Bill 79, including excluding certain sites (such as industrial employment hubs) from state requirements and allowing additional density and height for some residential projects near major transit. The Board also adopted an alternative plan intended to meet the state law’s capacity requirements.
This vote affects where new housing can be built near transit and where it will be limited, with long-term consequences for housing supply and neighborhood change. It also sets the City’s posture toward state housing mandates.
Finally passed 9-0 (with 2 supervisors excused).
PASSED
Curbside electric vehicle charging permit program approved
The Board authorized the San Francisco Municipal Transportation Agency to create a permit program for curbside electric vehicle charging stations on sidewalks. The legislation also clarified that permit holders would not need a separate sidewalk encroachment permit from the Department of Public Works and allowed administrative penalties for violations.
This sets up a citywide framework for expanding curbside charging, which could affect parking, sidewalk management, and access to electric vehicle infrastructure. Implementation details will be set through the permit program.
Finally passed 9-0 (with 2 supervisors excused).
