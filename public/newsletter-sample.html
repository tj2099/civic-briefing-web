The big picture
The Board approved a major, long-term financing and construction agreement for the Potrero Yard Modernization Project, including authority for up to $900 million in tax-exempt bonds, while urging agencies to pursue the affordable housing commitments tied to earlier approvals. Supervisors also advanced a $34.4 million shift into police overtime and moved several regulatory changes forward on first reading, including rules for compact mobile food operations and a police location-tracking policy. Land use actions were active as well, with special zoning districts approved at 1270 Mission Street and 2245 Post Street and key legal steps finalized for the Twin Peaks Promenade.
Top items
PASSED
Potrero Yard modernization approved, with $900 million bond authority
The Board approved a major project agreement for the San Francisco Municipal Transportation Agency’s Potrero Yard Modernization Project, covering design, construction, and financing of a new bus storage and maintenance facility. The resolution also approved the issuance of up to $900M in tax-exempt bonds through the California Municipal Finance Authority to help finance the project.
This is a long-term, high-cost infrastructure commitment tied to the City’s core transit operations, with payments projected over decades. The Board also formally urged the Mayor’s Office of Housing and Community Development and the San Francisco Municipal Transportation Agency to pursue funding and sites to deliver 465 affordable housing units referenced in earlier approvals.
Adopted as amended (10-0; President Rafael Mandelman voted “no” on the amendment but “yes” on final adoption).
IN PROGRESS
Police overtime budget shift of $34.4 million advanced
The Board moved forward a budget change that shifts $34.4M within the Police Department from regular salary categories into overtime pay to cover projected overtime needs in fiscal year 2025–2026.
The vote signals the City expects elevated overtime spending and is reallocating funds to match that reality. Final approval would lock in how the Police Department pays for staffing demands in the current fiscal year.
Passed on first reading (10-0).
PASSED
Twin Peaks Promenade street changes and land transfer approved
The Board approved a conditional street vacation and redesignation of portions of Christmas Tree Point Road and the eastern alignment of Twin Peaks Boulevard, shifting the right-of-way from roadway use to recreation and park purposes. It also approved transferring the affected property from Public Works to the Recreation and Park Department and clarified that the promenade must remain public open space in perpetuity under a grant restriction.
This clears a major legal and administrative step for the Twin Peaks Promenade Project, changing how the space is managed and what it can be used for. It also formalizes long-term public access requirements for the site.
Finally passed (10-0).
PASSED
Mission and 9th special zoning district re-established
The Board re-adopted zoning rules for the Mission and 9th Street Special Use District at 1270 Mission Street and increased the allowable height limit for qualifying projects from 120-X to 200-X.
Special zoning rules and a higher height limit can reshape what gets built on a key Mid-Market site, influencing housing and development capacity. The action also reaffirms the City’s environmental review determinations for the district.
Finally passed (10-0).
PASSED
New special zoning district approved at 2245 Post Street
The Board created the 2245 Post Street Special Use District and updated the Planning Code and Zoning Map accordingly, alongside required findings under state environmental review and the City’s General Plan.
Special use districts set tailored development rules for a specific site, affecting what can be built and under what conditions. The decision can influence neighborhood-scale housing and land-use outcomes at this address.
Finally passed (10-0).
