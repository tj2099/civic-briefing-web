San Francisco Weekly Briefing
Board of Supervisors · Week of March 27, 2026
The big picture
The Board advanced the Potrero Yard Modernization Project’s financing and delivery plan, including approval tied to up to $900 million in bonds, while formally urging steps to restore affordable-housing commitments connected to the site. Supervisors also moved $34.37 million into Police Department overtime on first reading and approved major land-use actions at Mission and 9th, 2245 Post, and Twin Peaks. Several policy items—including police location-tracking surveillance rules and new mobile food operation definitions—now head to final votes in the weeks ahead.
Top items
PASSED
Potrero Yard modernization deal advances with $900 million bonds
The Board approved a major long-term agreement to design, build, and finance the San Francisco Municipal Transportation Agency’s Potrero Yard bus storage and maintenance facility. The action sets the project’s financing framework, including approval for up to $900M in tax-exempt bond issuance through the California Municipal Finance Authority, and it formally notes the Board’s concerns about a reduced affordable-housing component tied to the broader site plan.
Adopted as amended; the amendment passed 9-1 and final approval was unanimous (10-0).
IN PROGRESS
Police overtime budget shift of $34.37 million moves forward
The Board advanced a midyear budget change that moves $34.4M from regular salary lines into overtime for the Police Department to cover projected overtime needs in fiscal year 2025–2026. This is a significant operational funding decision with citywide public-safety implications.
Passed on first reading, 10-0.
PASSED
Twin Peaks Promenade project clears street re-designation and transfer
Supervisors approved a conditional change that shifts portions of roadway space at Twin Peaks from transportation use to recreation and park use to enable the Twin Peaks Promenade Project. The ordinance also transfers specified property from Public Works to the Recreation and Park Department and applies park rules and excavation permitting authority to the area.
Finally passed, 10-0.
PASSED
Mission and 9th special zoning district restored, height raised
The Board re-established the Mission and 9th Street Special Use District at 1270 Mission Street and increased the height limit for compliant projects from 120 feet to 200 feet. The change is aimed at shaping redevelopment at a key site near transit and downtown.
Finally passed, 10-0.
PASSED
New special zoning district approved for 2245 Post Street
Supervisors created a new Special Use District at 2245 Post Street, updating zoning controls for that site. Special Use Districts can set tailored rules for development, affecting housing, design, and neighborhood impacts.
Finally passed, 10-0.
